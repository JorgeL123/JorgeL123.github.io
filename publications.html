Master Thesis

Numerical Relativity studies of spin induction and dynamical capture in dense clusters of black holes

 07/2025

Supervisors: Prof. Juan García-Bellido and Dr. Santiago Jaraba.

Abstract:

"The first direct detection of gravitational waves by LIGO in 2015 marked a revolutionary breakthrough in our ability to study the Universe. Over the past decade, the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA collaboration has identified over 80 gravitational-wave events, primarily originating from the mergers of compact objects such as black holes. These discoveries have significantly enhanced our understanding of astrophysical binaries, relativistic dynamics, and the nature of strong-field gravity.

Throughout this Thesis, I use Numerical Relativity simulations to investigate several relativistic phenomena that, while not yet observed by current interferometers, are expected to become increasingly relevant as detector sensitivity improves in the coming years. First, I will present previous studies on spin induction caused by hyperbolic encounters in dense clusters of black holes, examining how such interactions can influence the spin dynamics of compact objects. Additionally, I will explore the properties of dynamical captures — close hyperbolic encounters that can lead to an almost head-on collision after an effect of tidal capture. By analyzing these scenarios, this work aims to provide theoretical predictions that will help guide future gravitational-wave observations and deepen our understanding of relativistic astrophysics."
